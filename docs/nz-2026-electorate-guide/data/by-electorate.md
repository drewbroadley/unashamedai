## Auckland Central (General Roll · Auckland & Northland)

- **Towns & Suburbs (2025 Final Boundaries):** Auckland Central covers Auckland’s city centre, suburbs such as Ponsonby, Newton and Arch Hill, as well as the islands of the Hauraki Gulf.
- **Selectable Town/Suburb Groups:** Auckland CBD, Wynyard & Grafton (Auckland Central) · Ponsonby, Herne Bay, St Marys Bay & Freemans Bay (Auckland Central) · Grey Lynn, Westmere, Newton & Eden Terrace (Auckland Central) · Waiheke Island & Great Barrier / Aotea (Auckland Central)
- **Overview:** Auckland Central covers Auckland’s city centre, suburbs such as Ponsonby, Newton and Arch Hill, as well as the islands of the Hauraki Gulf. It is currently held by Green party co-leader Chlöe Swabrick, who won the electorate with a margin of 16,624 votes and has held the seat since 2020. National won the party vote in 2023 with 33%, followed by the Green Party on 24%. Barring one three year gap, the seat was held continuously by Labour from 1919-2008, but it has not regained it since.

## Coromandel (General Roll · Waikato, Bay of Plenty & Central North Island)

- **Towns & Suburbs (2025 Final Boundaries):** Coromandel covers the Coromandel peninsula, the Hauraki district, and some of the Bay of Plenty, finishing at the edge of Tauranga.
- **Selectable Town/Suburb Groups:** Thames, Coromandel Town, Whitianga, Tairua & Whangamatā (Coromandel) · Paeroa, Ngatea & Hauraki Plains (Coromandel) · Waihi, Waihi Beach, Katikati & Ōmokoroa (Coromandel)
- **Overview:** Coromandel covers the Coromandel peninsula, the Hauraki district, and some of the Bay of Plenty, finishing at the edge of Tauranga. Its main population centres include towns such as Thames, Waihi, and Katikati. National’s Scott Simpson has held the electorate since 2011, being re-elected in 2023 with a margin of 24,914 votes. National also won the party vote in 2023 with 43% of the vote, followed by Labour on 20%. The seat has only ever been held by National in the modern era, barring a three year stint where former Green Party leader Jeanette Fitzsimon eked out a 200-vote victory (Labour did not campaign for the electorate vote that year).
