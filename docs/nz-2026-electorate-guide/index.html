How MMP Works & Primary Sources

Why splitting your vote works in New Zealand

1. Party Vote sets total seats

All 120 seats in Parliament are allocated proportionally by the nationwide Party Vote (for parties crossing 5% or winning an electorate seat). Voting for a local MP from another party never subtracts seats from your Party Vote choice.

2. The “2-for-1” local MP bonus

When a senior MP is ranked high on their party list, they enter Parliament even if they lose the local seat. If a challenger who needs an electorate win beats them locally, your town gets two MPs in Parliament.

3. Coat-tailing (1-seat threshold)

Under the Electoral Act 1993, winning just one electorate seat waives the 5% threshold and brings a party’s full Party Vote share into Parliament — crucial for Te Pāti Māori, Te Tai Tokerau Party, and Opportunity (TOP).
